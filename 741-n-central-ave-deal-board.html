What we would ask for

  1. A fixed buy priceA price set now, not "whatever it appraises for later". A backup is the lower of the two.
  2. The right to rent out single roomsWithout asking the owner each time.
  3. The owner keeps the roof, structure and major systemsYou pay the NNN costs, with a cap on the upkeep part.
  4. A recorded notice of your optionFiled with the county so a later buyer or lender is bound by it.
  5. If they won't set a price: a right of first refusalYou get to match any offer. This is weaker, because it does not lock a price.

About your credits and the loan. Most lenders will not count rent credits as your down payment. They treat them as a lower price. The building still has to appraise at the set price. Plan to bring about 10% of the net price in cash when you buy: about $30,000 to $31,000 on these numbers (estimate).

Questions for your attorney: How should the lease and the option be written as separate Georgia documents? When and how should the option notice be recorded? Does the option bind the trust if the person behind it dies? Could a large fee or credit make a court or the IRS treat this as a sale or a loan?
