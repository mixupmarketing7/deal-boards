02Market read

Four independent signals. All four point the same direction.

a · The subdivision stopped building nine years ago

D.R. Horton bought lots here at $27,000 in 2015–16 and delivered homes at $170K–$215K through 2017. Then it stopped. Since 2017 there have been zero new-construction permits on Spring Creek Dr — the only permit pulled on the street since is a 3-ton HVAC change-out in April 2026.

b · Fifteen platted lots still have no structure

Nine on Spring Creek Dr (one is HOA common area), six on Woodlands Dr. Two unbuilt lots were absorbed by neighbours as side yards — 151-3-032 combined to 0.92 ac, 151-3-036 to 0.46 ac. And 3206 Woodlands sold in Nov 2025 for $34,000 to the adjacent homeowner: an assemblage buy, not a builder buy.

d · Resale value here sits below replacement cost

Existing 2,300–2,750 sf homes trade at $109–$128/sf. The build costs $132–$178/sf. That is the mathematical core of the problem — and it means every extra square foot costs ~$132 to build while adding ~$50 of appraised value. Building bigger makes it worse.

Dual exit — land as the floor

The lot's own resale value is the floor, and it is roughly the ask — with an identical lot next door at the same price and a seller who cut 56% in six weeks. No floor, no cushion.

Pre-sold custom for a buyer

Transfers the ARV risk but not the problem. A construction-to-perm lender appraises as-completed; at $132–$146/sf cost against $109–$128/sf comps, the appraisal gap becomes the buyer's cash-in — and the deal dies at underwriting instead of at resale.

07Phase 0 — ranked by dollar swing per dollar spent

Only worth running if you are still inclined to build after the above. Cheapest and highest-leverage first, all inside the due-diligence period.

  1. HOA architectural covenants — $0 Minimum square footage, masonry percentage, garage, roof pitch. Deal-killing: D.R. Horton-era covenants often mandate 1,800+ sf and masonry fronts, which outlaws the only plan that approaches break-even.
  2. Augusta Utilities sewer availability + tap fee letter — $0 Swing $10K–$18K. Septic plus drainfield on 0.228 ac may not even be feasible.
  3. R-1A minimum lot area & setbacks — $0 Lot is 10,019 sf. Confirm buildable envelope and lot-of-record status. Swing: the whole deal.
  4. Two real GC bids on a specific plan — $0–$500 Swing $40K+. This is the largest modeled, unverified number in the underwrite.
  5. Appraiser “as-completed” feasibility letter — $400–$600 Swing $30K–$50K, and it is what your lender will use anyway. Buy this before plans.
  6. Soils / tree survey — $500–$1,200 Swing $5K–$15K in fill, undercut and clearing.
  7. Title + plat: drainage easements — $200–$400 Swing $5K–$20K.

Resolved Flood zone — already checked at $0. FEMA NFHL returns Zone X, area of minimal flood hazard.

08Seller gap

They are asking $17,500. True value as a build site is ≤ $0 on this evidence. As a speculative land hold, comps are thin and mostly non-arm's-length: $34,000 (Nov 2025, adjacent owner), $30,000 (2020), $27,000 (2016, D.R. Horton).

With 15 unbuilt lots, five actively listed and a seller who cut 56% in six weeks, $8,000–$11,000 is the only defensible bid — and there is no exit at the other end. No meeting point exists that fixes this, because the lot price was never the problem.

09What the data says to do instead

1 · Buy below replacement, don't build above market

An existing D.R. Horton home here at ~$275K / 2,395 sf ($115/sf) trades at roughly 70–80% of what it costs to replace. That is the trade this dataset actually supports.

2 · If you want a new-build play in 30815, buy where absorption exists

Beazley's Southampton (Inverness / Hollington, $256K–$332K) and Stockport / Ashton ($376K–$424K), or Keystone's Pine Ridge ($302K–$405K). Those clear at $168+/sf with a builder's marketing behind them. Stranded lots in a dead subdivision are cheap for a reason.

3 · Read the D.R. Horton tell

The lowest-cost homebuilder in America bought lots here at $27,000, built through 2017, walked, and is now building townhomes at Laurel Park on Firestone Dr ($222K–$261K) instead. When the cheapest builder in the country won't build on a lot, the lot price isn't the reason.
