What his letter covers, and what it leaves out

In the letter

  • Review the survey and photos
  • A layout site plan for the homeowner to approve, then for the permit
  • Site visits during design and construction
  • Checking the work was built to the plans

Not in the letter

  • Wall section, footing through cap
  • Footing size, rebar size and spacing
  • Pier and veneer-tie details
  • Seal or structural calculations
  • A revision round if the City comments
  • Drawing delivery date
  • Payment terms
  • Homeowner’s right to use the plans
  • Proof of E&O insurance

His letter calls this a “cantilevered masonry wall,” which is engineering language. But what he promises to deliver is a site plan and site visits. Your crew builds from the wall section and the rebar schedule, and right now no one is on the hook to produce them.

Five things to fix

  1. Seal and calculations: in or out, in writing.You already asked on Sep 18. Keep asking until he answers.
  2. Place the wall from the survey line, not the old fence.The survey shows the existing fence up to 2.1 ft past the east property line, on the neighbor’s side. If the wall goes where the fence is, it sits on the neighbor’s land. The wall and its 30-inch footing need to sit inside the line, and Roger’s site plan should show the distance.
  3. Have Roger set the footing width.The masonry industry’s design table for a 6-ft fence wall (NCMA TEK 14-16B) allows a 30-inch footing only in the lightest wind case. Stronger wind calls for 34 or 38 inches, and so do wall ends without a return corner, like the pedestrian opening. Our rebar spec (#4 bars every 32 inches) already meets the table’s strongest case. Going from 30 to 34 inches adds about 0.6 cubic yards of concrete.
  4. Fix the homeowner paperwork.Rider R-1004-01 still names Denise. It also promises the homeowner three things Roger’s letter doesn’t give you: the right to use the plans, one revision round for City comments, and a professional standard of care. It needs an amended rider (R-1004-02) that names Roger. Exhibit A carries $1,200 for design, so EmpowerBuild absorbs the $2,050 difference. No change order goes to the owner.
  5. Fix the name and address, and get his W-9 and insurance.His letter uses the wrong company name and address, which you already flagged. Get a W-9, since you’ll owe him a 1099 in January. Also get an E&O certificate. Our general liability policy won’t cover a design mistake on this wall, and his professional liability would.

Could a drafter have done it? Probably. The industry table covers this wall without an engineer, and Indiana leaves it to the City to decide which drawings to require. With Roger already signed, though, what you get from a PE is the seal, so make sure you get it.

Your construction price holds up. The signed $43,650 works out to about $146 per square foot of wall face, or $873 per foot of wall. Published installed prices for brick masonry fences run $90–175+ per square foot. You’re inside the range.
