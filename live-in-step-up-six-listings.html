Verdict — Two fit, one maybe

Buy, live in, then step up

An owner-occupant buys with 3.5% down, lives there at least 12 months, then moves to the next home and keeps this one as a rental on the original loan. Six listings in 30349, 30318, 30316, 30311 and 30314, ranked for that play.

Internal working document. Contains price views, monthly costs and seller analysis. For Alvin Slate and deal partners only. Not for any listing agent or seller.

1174 Brookstone Rd is the best fit, and 5770 Deerfield Trl is second. Both cost about what renting the same house costs, and neither needs much cash. None of the six pays for itself as a rental at today's rates, so the step-up is an equity play: the owner carries a few hundred dollars a month after moving out, and gets the equity and a tenant-paid loan in return.

Worked example, 1174 Brookstone. Buy the bank-approved short sale at $215,000 with FHA at 3.5% down. Cash to close is about $16,975 before any seller credit. Living there costs about $1,902 a month, against $1,790 to rent the same house. It is worth about $235,000, so the buyer starts with about $17,000 of equity. After moving out, rent of $1,790 leaves the owner about $603 a month short with a manager, or about $468 self-managed. Equity at month 24 is about $38,000.

03What has to be true for each one

1174 Brookstone Rd — short sale, Crystal Lake, Clayton County

FMLS 7784705. Vacant, bank-approved short sale at $215,000 (original $270,000, 106 days). Sold for $265,000 in 12/2023. Roof 2021, water heater 2022. A past leak in the loft was repaired, but the ceiling is not painted. Before an offer: pull Crystal Lake closed sales to confirm the $235,000 value, get the short-sale approval letter's terms, and confirm Clayton County schools and taxes ($3,473 on the 2025 bill; about $4,085 once the homestead ends).

5770 Deerfield Trl — institutional seller, five cuts

GAMLS 10763245. Livable now with central heat and air; the repair scope ($46,495 likely) is roof, exterior paint and siding, systems service and lower-level work. The county counts 3 bedrooms; the two lower rooms need egress to count. A 203(k) consultant and an inspection set the real scope. The seller has cut from $204,900 to $174,900, so an owner-occupant offer near $150,000 to $160,000 is worth trying. The full board covers the condition and comps.

1761 Liberty Pkwy NW — gated Westside townhouse

FMLS 7839841. Condo ownership, FHA-approved complex, dues $460 a month including exterior insurance, pool and gate. The unit leased before at $1,200 (2012), $1,525 (2017) and $1,500 (2019), but the listing now marks a rent restriction. If the rules cap or bar leasing, the step-up fails here. The seller and listing agent are related, and the seller is motivated.

141 Walnut St SW — the house hack

FMLS 7847608. Living in one unit and renting the other on an FHA 2-unit loan is the classic version of this play, and it brings the owner's cost to about $1,546 a month. It does not work at $300,000: the value is about $185,000 to $240,000, and the duplex use is lost if both units have sat empty 12 months. It reopens near $240,000 with the zoning confirmed.

2702 Rollingwood Ln SE and 2453 Ivydale Dr SW

Rollingwood is a finished flip at retail ($299,000 against a $293,000 model value), so there is no equity to start with; the seller's lender advertises a 6% rate, which would help but not fix it. Ivydale needs about $100,000 of cash on top of the loan and a City flood answer first.

04Before any offer — checks in order

  1. Brookstone: Crystal Lake closed sales and the short-sale approval letter.Cost $0. Confirms the $17,000 starting equity and the price the bank already approved.
  2. Liberty Park: the association's leasing rules in writing.Cost $0 from the listing agent or management company. A ban or a long wait list ends the step-up.
  3. Deerfield: inspection plus a 203(k) consultant walk-through.About $600 to $1,000. Sets the repair scope that drives both the loan and the after-repair value.
  4. Buyer's loan quote: FHA rate, mortgage insurance and the seller-credit limit.Cost $0. Every number on this board moves with the rate.
  5. Next-home plan.FHA usually allows one owner-occupied FHA loan at a time, so the next home is likely conventional. Lenders typically count a share of the signed lease on the first home toward the next loan.
