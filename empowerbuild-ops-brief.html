Demolition, re-priced against the Indianapolis market

The line is $16,065.00, not the $10,425 quoted earlier in this brief. That earlier figure missed the basketball goals line — six goals, backboards and mounting structures at $940.00 each, $5,640.00, with anchors patched and sealed. That line is properly priced and sits inside the market band, so it stays.

The tiered platform is the line to look at. It is carried at $11.25 per square foot across 660 square feet. Indianapolis contractor cost for wood-framed platform demolition runs about $6.05 to $8.35 per square foot, so $11.25 reads high — unless the tiers are poured concrete, in which case it reads low, because concrete tiers are a volume job and the same 660 square feet could cost $8,000 to $12,500 to take out. Wood or concrete is the single fact that moves this line by about $7,000, and nobody has written it down. Worth confirming before the document goes back to the church.

The $6,500 outside bid is probably real. At Indianapolis burdened demolition wages the cost floor for the platform and stage alone is roughly $5,800 to $8,100 before disposal, so $6,500 is coherent if that sub is filling dumpsters you are already paying for and keeping the scrap steel. Test it on scope, not on price: does it include the six basketball goals or only the platform and stage; is disposal his or yours; risers as well as the stage; dust containment for an occupied church; and is he a licensed wrecking contractor, bonded and insured naming the Consolidated City of Indianapolis. That last one is where cheap demo subs usually fail.

One box too many. The scope generates roughly 55 to 76 loose cubic yards, and a 40-yard box realistically holds 26 to 32 of bulky demolition debris. Three roll-offs, not four. The general conditions line carries four; dropping one frees about $650 to $700 inside it.

Two regulatory things nobody has priced

Asbestos is not optional and it has a clock on it. Indiana requires an inspection by a licensed asbestos inspector before demolition or renovation at any facility except non-commercial residential of four units or fewer, regardless of the building's age — a church gymnasium is not exempt. A notification also has to be filed with IDEM ten working days before work starts, which constrains mobilisation, not just paperwork. That is why an allowance for the inspection and notification has been added to EST-4625-02. Abatement is written in as an exclusion: if the survey finds asbestos-containing floor tile or mastic under the seating platform, commercial abatement on 660 square feet runs somewhere between roughly $7,900 and $19,800, and that has to be a change order rather than a surprise.

The decision to skip permits deserves a second look. Taking the permit allowance out was your call and it is reflected in the document. But converting a gymnasium into a sanctuary changes the occupant load and the egress requirements of an assembly space — that is not the same as an ordinary interior refresh, and it is the kind of change a plan reviewer expects to see. Worth a phone call to Indianapolis DBNS before the church is told no permit is being pulled. The permit itself is cheap; discovering it was needed after the partition is up is not.

One thing that is not a risk: prevailing wage. Indiana repealed the Common Construction Wage Act in 2015, and private church work with no federal money carries no Davis-Bacon obligation either — unless the church used a federal grant on this project, which is worth one question.
