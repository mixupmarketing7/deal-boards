01 · The product problem

A score is easy to publish. A trustworthy product is harder.

The original market-leverage concept combined four public listing signals into a ZIP-level rank. The product challenge was to decide what the number could honestly mean, make it reproducible, stabilize it for recurring use, and create a path toward proprietary value without hiding the method or violating data rights.

What existed

A transparent but noisy market signal.

The public score was recomputable from Realtor.com Economic Research data and useful as an editorial front door, but the formula was not a defensible moat and month-to-month movement could undermine trust.

What the product needed

A governed release system.

Freeze the method, preserve historical outputs, publish the right disclosures, measure stability, and separate public descriptive signals from private outcome calibration.

02 · Discovery and decision-making

The work moved from “make it proprietary” to “make it citable.”

1

Reproduce the existing signal

Recomputed all 375 Indianapolis ZIP-months exactly and reproduced the Atlanta published universe through the warehouse checks. Reproducibility became the baseline, not a side effect.

2

Measure the credibility risks

Tested stability, cross-50 flips, top-10 overlap, forward ZHVI relationship, quality flags, and competitive overlap. The result: the signal was real enough to study, but too noisy to headline without smoothing.

3

Choose the smallest high-value improvement

A three-month trailing signal lifted adjacent-month stability from roughly 0.795 to 0.937 over the ten-year Atlanta panel. Equal weights stayed frozen until a licensed outcome target exists.

4

Define the moat as coupling and validation

The defensible product is not a secret public formula. It is the operated series, the validation ledger, the private FMLS calibration path, and the lawful connection to PRE assessment outcomes.

03 · Product architecture

One public layer, one private calibration path, one monthly operating rhythm.

Public layer

Negotiating Room

A within-metro, within-month rank using four named listing signals. Higher means more of the conditions that usually give buyers room to negotiate. A rank, not a forecast or discount.

Context layer

Assessment Watch

Georgia-only public-record context combining market valuation drift, county ratio study signals, appeal calendar timing, and county-level evidence without publishing parcel-level dollars.

Private layer

Your Two Numbers

A one-address card inside the homeowner report: assessment gap, county-only win-rate evidence where the sample supports it, savings interval, appeal window, and market-room band.

Operating layer

Monthly release discipline

Source vintage, file hash, universe size, quality flags, golden regression, validation ledger, changelog, dashboard, email, and downstream distribution.

04 · Evidence and validation

The product earned confidence by showing its limits.

Key result: the three-month smoothed version improved the ten-year Atlanta adjacent-month stability measure to 0.937, while the frozen v1.0 golden checks reproduced 901 Atlanta and 375 Indianapolis ZIP-months with zero mismatches.

06 · Interview framing

“I turned a clever score into a governed product.”

Short version: “I took a public-data market signal from a one-off dashboard toward a citable product. I reverse-engineered and regression-tested the existing output, measured where it could mislead, used smoothing to improve stability, froze the public method, and designed a roadmap that keeps licensed outcomes and tax-appeal calibration in a private layer.”
Methods used

Product discovery, quantitative validation, data lineage, versioned golden testing, data-rights review, staged roadmap design, and operating-model design.

Artifacts produced

Negotiating Room naming brief, C-suite model critique, v2 specification, validation gates, release roadmap, warehouse governance decisions, and public dashboard/email product direction.
