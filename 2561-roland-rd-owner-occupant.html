Gate 1 — boundary: RESOLVED

The county parcel map settles it, and it settles favorably. Reading the qPublic overlay for parcel 025 039:

Caveat: this is a GIS overlay on orthoimagery, not a survey. County polygons routinely carry 10–30 feet of positional error. It retires the existential risk; it does not replace a boundary survey, which you should still order during due diligence at $600–$1,500.

Reaching the rendered result means building at a new site near the lake, with a new driveway and a new utility run: that is new construction, not an addition, and it costs more than the numbers below.

Gate 2 — sewage, and therefore financing

The MLS lists sewer as “Holding Tank” — a pump-out vessel, not a permanent waste system for a three-bedroom dwelling under Georgia DPH rules. The county Permits module is empty: no septic permit, no soil record, nothing in 51 years of ownership.

FHA, VA and USDA RD all require a permitted system acceptable to the local health authority, and most conventional lenders do too. No permittable system means no financing — on your purchase and on any future sale. USDA Rural Development, which is otherwise built for exactly this property, is unavailable until this clears.

Cost if permittable: $18,000 standard install. $25,000–$40,000 if soils fail or lake setbacks and Soil Productivity 4 force an engineered / advanced-treatment system — plus perc testing and county permitting time.

Financing paths if the tank cannot be cured before closing:

  • FHA 203(k) or Fannie Mae HomeStyle Renovation — finances purchase and the septic install together
  • Local Upson / Middle-Georgia bank portfolio or construction-to-perm loan
  • Cash, cure the system, then rate-and-term refinance

Cross-check: indexing the seller's 1975 purchase

The sellers bought in April 1975 for $39,000 (Deed Book 244/88,90, county reason code “fair market value”). Indexing that forward:

Series1975Q12026Q2Multiple$39,000 becomes
FHFA All-Transactions HPI, Georgia (observed)73.88662.088.96×$349,501

4.39% compounded over 51 years. Source: FHFA quarterly all-transactions state index, hpi_at_state.csv, downloaded 2026-09-18.

Do not use this as a value. It overstates by roughly $113,000, and here is exactly why:
  • The index assumes maintenance. Repeat-sales indices track houses that were kept up. This one has zero permits in 51 years and the listing says it needs repairs and updating. You are indexing an asset that did not hold its condition.
  • Statewide Georgia is metro Atlanta. Thomaston is a separate micropolitan area 60 miles south that has not tracked it. The proof is in this same file: ZIP 30286 median sale is −4.0% year over year while the state index keeps climbing.
  • Wrong asset mix. The index basket is houses on ordinary lots. This is 1,425 sf of house on 16 acres — mostly land, and land does not appreciate on the housing index.
  • 51 years compounds error. A half-point annual drift over that span moves the answer by nearly 30%.
Where the 1975 price IS useful — and it is genuinely useful. It tells you about the seller, not the property. A $39,000 basis held 51 years means there is almost certainly no mortgage, so no payoff sets a price floor. At a $150,000 contract they still book roughly a $111,000 gain. Nothing in their financial position forces them to hold out for the ask.

It also tells you what you are negotiating against: a seller anchored on a half-century of appreciation and a county assessment that rose 126% in five years. Expect the first counter to reflect that story rather than the comps.
