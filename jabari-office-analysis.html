If you want to lease part of it, none of these six work

You have said you prefer a freestanding building and intend to lease out some of the space. That is a sound plan, and it rules out both office condos on this list — condo bylaws commonly restrict subleasing, and the dues buy you no control.

But it also rules out the freestanding ones, for a simpler reason: they are too small. Your firm needs roughly 1,300 sf. These buildings are 1,162 to 1,684 sf. After you move in there is nothing left to lease.

The size your strategy actually needs

There is a ceiling as well as a floor, and the ceiling comes from the financing. Synovus is lending on an owner-occupied basis, and that kind of loan generally requires your business to occupy at least half the building. With your firm at 1,300 sf, that puts the maximum around 2,600 sf. Go bigger and the loan is re-classed as investment property: worse terms, more cash down.

The test that decides it

Take the rent you would otherwise pay for the same amount of space and divide it by the purchase price. That is your rent-saved yield. Compare it to the debt constant, which is what the loan costs each year as a percentage of the amount borrowed.

If the rent-saved yield sits below the debt constant, buying costs more than renting — and no amount of negotiating inside a realistic range fixes it. The building is simply priced above what that space is worth to occupy.

What each one means

3017 Piedmont Rd NE #200 — strongest on paper, one serious obstacle

Fourteen months on the market and three price cuts, from $379,000 down to $340,000. The economics rank first here.

The obstacle is the appraisal, not the price. There have been no closed office-condo sales within half a mile of this building in ten years. A lender making a 90% loan needs an appraisal, and an appraiser with no comparable sales may not be able to support the contract price. That gap would have to be covered in cash on top of the down payment.

Its ranking also assumes your alternative is leasing 1,416 sf of Buckhead space at $39/sf. If your real alternative is a shared suite closer to $2,400 a month, most of the advantage disappears.

1820 Mallard Cove, Suite 300 — best fit, if it is still available

The only one of the six that fits inside your stated cash. Positive leverage, the lowest monthly of the group, and it is in Cobb, which is where you have said the family is heading in two to three years.

Two things need confirming before an offer. The unit that came back in our research is Suite 300, which is marketed as fully leased — so we need to know which suite is actually for sale. And there is an unconfirmed special assessment of roughly $25,000, which is a one-time capital cost on top of the monthly dues.

2741 Church St — a coin flip before the foundation is priced

Over ten years, owning and leasing come out within about $8,000 of each other. That is a tie, and it is calculated before any structural repair is included.

For context on price: the parcel immediately next door, 2731 Church St, sold in July 2025 for $185,000, which works out to about $48 per square foot. This building is asking roughly four times that per foot.

1463 Church St and 2195 Fairburn Rd — the math does not work

At $409 and $386 per square foot, both are priced well above what that space rents for. Their rent-saved yields, 6.8% and 5.4%, fall below the 8.9% cost of the debt. That is negative leverage: owning costs more than leasing regardless of how the negotiation goes.

Both also sit above your cash-to-close ceiling. Fairburn has not had a single price reduction in eleven months.

1683 W Washington Ave — not enough information to score

The county has no recorded square footage for this parcel, and without it there is no way to compare it to anything else. Worth noting the county street name is West Washington Avenue rather than Washington Road.

What we need next
