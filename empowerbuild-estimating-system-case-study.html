One price, checked twice

Construction estimates used to live in someone's head and a blank template. Here's the pipeline that replaced that, and the errors it was built to stop happening again.

The problem

Every estimate started from a blank copy of the same template, priced by hand, with no single place a number had to be right. That looked fine until it didn't:

Caught before the client saw it

One job's per-trade subtotals were silently wrong through two full revisions before anyone noticed. The grand total happened to be right, so nothing looked broken until someone added the trade lines by hand.

Caught by a file-size check

A render silently overwrote a good, client-ready estimate PDF with a corrupted error page. Nothing in the workflow would have caught it without deliberately checking file size on every render.

Caught before signature

A signed contract sat roughly $6,450 under its likely cost because scope changed after pricing and nobody re-priced it. The gap was found on review, not by the client.

Caught by a document-control rule

A revised contract went out for e-signature twice within the same hour. The e-signature platform has no cancel/void tool, so a premature send has to be killed by hand. One rule now: nothing reaches signature without sign-off first.

The system

The fix wasn't a better template. It was making sure a price only has to be right in exactly one place, and that every step that could silently fail gets checked instead of assumed.

01

One underwrite per job

A single verified file drives every line. The client-facing rate is primary; internal cost derives from it, not the other way around.

02

Read scope, don't guess it

Design documents and scanned records get read for material and scope detail through AI-assisted extraction, checked against the underwrite before it's used. Dimensions are a different problem, solved in the next stage.

03

Assessor-card takeoffs

For Indianapolis jobs, wall heights and perimeters come off the county assessor's own property record card. That's data a photo or a footprint map can't give you.

04

Byte-size QC gate

Every rendered estimate gets checked before delivery. A healthy PDF runs 300–400KB; a silently-overwritten one lands near 46KB, and now that's caught automatically.

05

EST → CA → CO

Estimate, contract, and change order each get their own numbering track. A revision is always a new file, never an overwrite of the last one.

06

Stage before signature

Nothing goes out for e-signature until it's staged and reviewed first, closing the exact gap that caused the double-send.

Standing rules

  • Scope changes get a change order, not a renegotiation. Cost and schedule impact are recorded and approved by both the customer and ownership before unfunded work starts. No verbal approval or silence counts as authorization. This is the direct fix for the underpriced contract above.
  • 20% markup, every estimate. Set once, applied consistently, instead of negotiated case by case.
  • Geocode before you price. A bad address has broken two jobs already; now it's checked first, every time.
  • Never invent a unit cost. If a number isn't sourced, it's marked an allowance, not presented as a firm price.
